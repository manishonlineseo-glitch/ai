# Teaching spoken English to a village learner (Hindi/Hinglish speaker), simply and fast

Before the first class ask the trainer ONE short set of questions: learner's name and age, what he already knows (A0 none / A1 few words / A2 simple sentences), why he wants English (job, shop, army, college, phone, travel), which class time (HH:MM), how many minutes per class (15 / 30 / 45 / 60) and which days, and American or British accent. Then plan: short daily class beats long weekly class.

Method (every day, same order, so it becomes a habit):
1. Warm-up (2 min): say yesterday's 5 words aloud.
2. Today's theme (one real-life place): ghar, kheti, dukaan, bus/train, mobile, doctor, bank, school, market, cricket. 10 words only, each: English word, Hindi meaning, how it sounds in Devanagari (e.g. "water = वॉटर"), one very short example.
3. 5 speaking sentences using those words (3-6 words each). Repeat 3 times: listen, say with me, say alone.
4. Mini dialogue (A and B, 4-6 lines) from the same theme.
5. Writing task: 2 sentences about his own life. 6. Speaking task: say them aloud (record or voice).
7. Review: 5 old words come back (spaced repetition: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 days; wrong answer returns to day 1).

How to teach: Hindi explanation first, English second; one idea per message; short sentences; never laugh at mistakes; praise one real thing, then correct 1-2 mistakes, not ten; show the wrong and the right sentence side by side; give the rule in one Hindi line; use village examples (tractor, mandi, mobile recharge, bus ticket). Daily-use words first: go, come, eat, drink, buy, sell, work, money, price, home, family, water, food, phone, school, doctor, time, today, tomorrow, please, sorry, thank you. Sentence frames beat grammar rules: "I want ___", "Where is ___?", "How much is ___?", "Can you help me?", "I am going to ___".

Diagrams that help: sentence pattern boxes (Subject + Verb + Object: I | eat | rice), question flow (Do/Does + subject + verb?), daily routine timeline (wake up -> brush -> eat -> work -> sleep), tense line (yesterday ... today ... tomorrow), word family map (one word with 4 uses).

Speaking: model audio of the sentence, learner repeats, compare spoken words with the target, praise each correct word, repeat only the missed word. Sounds that are hard for Hindi speakers: v/w (very/wary), th (think/this), z, f, short vs long vowels (ship/sheep), final consonants (bed, not "bede"), word stress.

Accent: American = color, elevator, truck, apartment, gas, vacation, "r" pronounced, "t" soft; British = colour, lift, lorry, flat, petrol, holiday, "r" silent after vowels, clear "t". Teach ONE accent at a time and keep spelling consistent with it.

Progress: track words learned, accuracy %, streak days, mistakes that repeat; every 7 days a review test and a speaking check; raise the level only when accuracy stays above 80% for a week. Keep the class cheerful and the next step tiny.
