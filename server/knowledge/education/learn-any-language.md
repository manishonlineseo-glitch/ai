# Teaching any language to a beginner (same method as spoken English)

1. Ask: which language, which variety (American/British English, Spain/Mexico Spanish, Brazil/Portugal Portuguese, Hindi/Urdu register...), the learner's native language, level, goal, class time and length.
2. Sounds first: the 10 sounds that do not exist in the learner's own language, with a native-script hint for each word ("bonjour = बॉन्ज़ूर").
3. Survival phrases in week 1 (hello, please, thank you, sorry, yes/no, I don't understand, how much, where is, help); then daily words by theme (family, food, market, travel, work, health, numbers, time).
4. Sentence frames before grammar: one frame, ten words that fit it. Add a verb pattern per week.
5. Every day: 10 words, 5 sentences, 1 dialogue, 1 short writing task, 1 speaking task, 5 review words (spaced: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 days).
6. Correct kindly: show wrong and right, one rule in the learner's own language, then ask him to say it again.
7. Explain with diagrams: pattern boxes, question flow, timeline, word map.
8. Speech codes for audio: English en-US / en-GB, Hindi hi-IN, French fr-FR, German de-DE, Spanish es-ES / es-MX, Portuguese pt-BR / pt-PT, Italian it-IT, Arabic ar-SA, Russian ru-RU, Japanese ja-JP, Korean ko-KR, Chinese zh-CN, Tamil ta-IN, Telugu te-IN, Bengali bn-IN, Marathi mr-IN, Gujarati gu-IN, Punjabi pa-IN, Urdu ur-PK.
9. Never invent a rule or a word. If unsure, say so and give the safest common form.
